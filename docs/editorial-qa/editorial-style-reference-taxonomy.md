# ALTOS LAB Editorial Style Reference Taxonomy

This is an internal style map distilled from a 100+ outlet and columnist scan. It is a writing compass, not a claim that ALTOS LAB copies any outlet.

## What ALTOS LAB Should Borrow

- Reuters/AP: lead with what happened, who is affected and what changes next.
- Bloomberg/FT/WSJ: make the business consequence visible before the background.
- The Economist/HBR/McKinsey: turn the story into a decision frame, but avoid consultant fog.
- Wired/The Verge/TechCrunch: keep technical context readable and concrete.
- MIT Technology Review/Nature/Science: separate evidence, uncertainty and claim strength.
- Nikkei/Straits Times/CNA: respect regional business context and local decision habits.
- Longform magazines: use one scene or image to create tension, then earn the broader point.
- Newsletters: make the reader feel "I know what to do with this today."

## ALTOS LAB House Pattern

1. Open with a concrete decision, scene or source-backed event.
2. State the ALTOS LAB judgment early.
3. Explain the source trail without sounding like a bibliography.
4. Translate technical terms into operational meaning.
5. Add the tradeoff: cost, risk, timing, who should wait.
6. Give a short action path: what to check this week, who owns it, what to measure.
7. End with a useful test, not a slogan.

## Title And Subtitle Rules

- Title should contain a decision or tension, not only a topic.
- Subtitle should answer why the reader should care now.
- Avoid "完整解析", "懶人包", "趨勢不可忽視", "企業必看" and other generic traffic bait.
- Market news titles can be direct and source-led.
- Columns need a stronger angle: a claim, warning, inversion, operating rule or tradeoff.

## Section Variety

Use different shapes across articles so the blog does not feel templated:

- Scene hook: a moment where the risk appears.
- Source card: what one announcement actually proves.
- Decision map: choices and consequences.
- Failure rehearsal: what breaks first.
- Operator checklist: what the team should inspect.
- Contrarian note: what not to automate or buy yet.
- Local angle: what changes for Taiwan or SEA teams.
- Metric lens: what to watch after launch.

## Localization Rules

- Translate the decision, not only the sentence.
- Keep the same sources, media and article identity across languages.
- Use local business phrasing for Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia and the Philippines.
- Do not force Taiwanese idioms into SEA editions.
- If a concept has no natural local equivalent, explain it once in plain language.
