# ALTOS LAB AI Blog Market Writing Study

Date: 2026-05-31

Purpose: turn public AI media patterns into a practical writing bar for ALTOS
LAB. This is not a source pack for one article. It is an editorial calibration
document for Gemini, Antigravity, Codex review and the production quality gate.

## 2026-06-03 Research Expansion

Spark subagents sampled 155+ additional AI/tech/business articles across
regional media, official AI lab blogs and longform visual layouts. The sample
was pattern research, not a claim of exhaustive crawling.

- Regional/localization sample: 55+ Taiwan, Japan, Korea, Indonesia, Vietnam,
  Thailand, Malaysia and Philippines technology/business articles.
- Official AI lab sample: 50 official product/research/engineering posts from
  OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepMind, Google, Microsoft, Meta, Hugging Face and
  LangChain-style sources.
- Visual layout sample: 50 longform tech/business/AI articles using in-article
  images, diagrams, illustrations, screenshots or documentary photography.

Research conclusions now added to the production bar:

1. **Columns must be argument-led, not summary-led.** Start from a decision,
   risk, counterintuitive claim or market tension, then use sources to support
   the argument.
2. **Source cards matter.** A durable column should know what each source
   supports, what it does not prove, and what changed on what date.
3. **Citation-friendly writing is not keyword writing.** Use clear headings,
   facts, dates, source links, definitions and FAQ, but never make public copy
   sound like SEO machinery.
4. **Localization is editorial, not literal translation.** Market news keeps
   facts tight; ALTOS LAB columns can add local operating implications for
   Taiwan, Japan, Korea and Southeast Asia without inventing claims.
5. **In-article images need a job.** For columns/features, use 2-3 images:
   an opening anchor image after the nut graf, a mechanism/evidence image in
   the middle, and optionally a closing synthesis image before FAQ or sources.

Language notes:

- `zh-Hant`: concise, evidence-first, practical for Taiwan operators.
- `ja`: more formal and noun-led; avoid English sentence rhythm.
- `ko`: trust, safety, policy and enterprise-readiness language matters.
- `id`: direct and benefit-led; keep common AI terms where natural.
- `vi`: can be more conversational; keep technical terms clear but not stiff.
- `th`: business and policy framing often coexist; keep long sentences under control.
- `ms`: official/industry tone is common; use structured "purpose-principle-execution".
- `fil`: reader-first English/Filipino rhythm; avoid over-formal translation.

## Market Pattern

The better AI media pieces do not begin with "AI is changing X." They begin
with a live tension:

- A market rule has cracked.
- A trusted system is behaving strangely.
- A product launch changes a user's next decision.
- A research result creates an uncomfortable limit.
- A company move reveals how platform power is shifting.

Then they earn the analysis with named sources, clear categories, evidence,
quotes or tables, and a practical implication. SEO and GEO happen because the
piece is clear, structured and source-grounded, not because the article visibly
tries to feed an algorithm.

Google Search Central is especially important for ALTOS LAB: Google's official
position is that existing SEO fundamentals remain relevant for AI Overviews and
AI Mode, and there is no special AI markup required to appear. The writing bar
therefore cannot be "add GEO language." It must be people-first content that is
indexable, textual, internally linked, supported by high-quality media and
aligned with visible structured data.

## Opening Patterns Worth Copying

### 1. Thesis With Stakes

Use when the topic is a market shift.

Shape:

1. Name the old rule.
2. Name the thing that broke it.
3. Say what metric or decision changes.

ALTOS LAB example:

> For years, content teams optimized for the page that ranked. AI search changes
> the harder question: when the answer engine summarizes a market, will it cite
> the page, misread it, or skip it entirely?

### 2. Scene Hook

Use when the topic is operational.

Shape:

1. Put the reader inside a real decision.
2. Show the hidden failure mode.
3. Introduce the framework.

ALTOS LAB example:

> A marketing lead asks Gemini whether a vendor is credible. The answer sounds
> polished, but the cited source is an old event page. That is not only a search
> problem; it is a brand-memory problem.

### 3. Research Shock

Use when the topic has a paper or measurement study.

Shape:

1. State the surprising result.
2. Identify the study or source.
3. Say what should and should not be concluded.

ALTOS LAB example:

> A 2026 measurement study found that Google AI Overviews cite sources outside
> the visible first page and still leave some claims unsupported. The takeaway is
> not panic; it is that source quality and claim fidelity must be managed
> separately.

### 4. Product Launch Into Operator Decision

Use when the topic is a company announcement.

Shape:

1. Name the launch.
2. Translate it into a workflow implication.
3. Give the reader the next question to ask.

ALTOS LAB example:

> Google I/O 2026 did not only add AI features. It pushed Gemini deeper into
> Search, Workspace, Chrome and everyday tasks, which means teams need to decide
> which brand facts, workflows and source pages are ready to be interpreted by
> agents.

## Structure Patterns

### News Decode

Use for `breaking`.

Required sections:

- Direct answer: what happened and who is affected.
- Why it matters now.
- What remains uncertain.
- What to watch next.
- Source note.

Must include:

- 2+ credible sources.
- One "next watchpoint" paragraph.
- No forced sales pitch.

### Market Analysis

Use for market movement, platform power or buyer behavior.

Required sections:

- Core thesis.
- Supply / demand / distribution or platform lens.
- Stakeholder impact table.
- Scenario map: optimistic, base, cautious.
- ALTOS LAB judgment and next operator action.

Must include:

- Named variables, not vague trends.
- One table or chart.
- Clear uncertainty.

### Deep Feature

Use for durable assets.

Required sections:

- Reader problem.
- Mechanism explainer.
- Evidence and source trail.
- Framework or scorecard.
- Risks and failure modes.
- Practical implementation path.
- FAQ.

Must include:

- 4+ credible sources.
- One comparison table.
- One repeatable decision rule.

### Operator Column

Use for a strong ALTOS LAB point of view.

Required sections:

- Tempting mistake.
- Why smart teams make it.
- Better rule.
- Tradeoff table.
- What to do this week.

Must include:

- An quotable judgment.
- The best opposing argument.
- Practical next steps.

## SEO And GEO Without Killing The Article

Keep:

- Clear H1/H2 hierarchy.
- Direct answer in the first 40-80 words.
- Named sources with URLs and dates where possible.
- Visible FAQ.
- Definitions for technical terms.
- Tables, checklists and decision rules.
- Natural keyword usage based on the topic.
- High-quality, topic-specific cover image and alt text.

Avoid:

- Turning the article into a keyword page.
- Adding GEO claims that Google does not support.
- Treating `llms.txt`, schema or FAQ as a magic ranking lever.
- Overstating one study as a universal platform rule.
- Source summaries with no ALTOS LAB judgment.

## Image Direction Learned From Market References

Good AI cover images usually have one idea and one visual anchor. They look like
editorial artifacts, not generic technology wallpaper.

Use:

- Research desk, source cards, annotated documents or physical diagrams.
- One clean data artifact, not a dashboard full of fake metrics.
- Neutral palette with one accent.
- Strong negative space for headline safe area.
- No people unless the article is truly about a person and rights are clear.

Reject:

- AI brain, robot handshake, neon grid, fake command center, stock business
  meeting, fake UI, text artifacts, logos, trademarks or real faces.
- Abstract tech backgrounds that could fit any AI article.
- Images that need the title to explain the subject.

## ALTOS LAB Editorial Gate v2

Hold the draft unless it passes all checks:

- Opening gate: first 40-80 words contain the article conclusion, the key
  variable, and the next watchpoint or reader decision.
- Source gate: facts, official claims, analysis and uncertainty are visually or
  verbally separable.
- Reader gate: the piece contains at least one table, checklist, decision rule,
  quote box, field note or scenario map.
- Judgment gate: one paragraph must be unmistakably ALTOS LAB's view, not a
  rewritten source.
- GEO humility gate: the article does not imply secret AI-search tricks or
  unsupported ranking guarantees.
- Image gate: the cover has one subject, no protected brands or people, no fake
  text/UI, and remains understandable as a mobile thumbnail.

## Prompt Rules To Feed Gemini

Use this before drafting:

```text
請沿用前文，不要重置上下文。這次先把文章寫得值得讀，再處理 SEO/GEO。

請先選一種寫法：thesis with stakes / scene hook / research shock / product launch into operator decision。

開頭 40-80 字必須同時包含：
1. 本文結論
2. 關鍵變數
3. 下一個觀察點或讀者要做的決策

文章中必須清楚分開：
- 來源事實
- 官方立場
- ALTOS LAB 判斷
- 不確定性
- 讀者下一步

SEO title、meta、FAQ、GEO summary 只能服務文章，不可以變成文章骨架。
```
